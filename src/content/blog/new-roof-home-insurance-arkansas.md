## Does a new roof lower your home insurance in Arkansas?

It can, but it depends on your insurer and on what kind of roof you put on. We found no Arkansas law that requires a discount just because a roof is new. The one discount state law requires is for homes certified to the FORTIFIED standard, when the discount is actuarially justified, on policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2026.

That rule comes from Act 427 of 2025, the Strengthen Arkansas Homes Program Act. It is new, and most homeowners have not heard of it yet.

Here is how the three common cases compare.

| Your new roof | Does Arkansas law require a discount? | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard shingle or metal roof | None that we found | Ask your agent what they credit |
| Impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles | None that we found. It is insurer by insurer | Ask before you pick the shingle |
| FORTIFIED-certified roof | Yes, if the discount is actuarially justified | Send the certificate to your insurer |

## What is the FORTIFIED discount law?

It is a section of Arkansas law that covers insurers who write wind or hail coverage. If a home has been certified to the FORTIFIED Home standard, the insurer must give a premium discount or rate reduction when that discount is actuarially justified. The law sets no percentage.

The insurer has to apply it once it has a copy of the certificate. The rule covers policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2026.

The same act added a second rule. Insurers must offer an optional add-on to the policy for homes that are not FORTIFIED. With that add-on, a covered claim that requires a new roof also pays toward upgrading it to the FORTIFIED standard. Insurers must offer it on policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2026, and each insurer sets the cost and the limits.

## What is a FORTIFIED roof?

It is a roof built to a standard that goes beyond the building code. The program is run by the Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety, a research group funded by insurers. It targets the three places a roof fails in wind: the deck connections, the edges, and the gaps in the deck.

Two things about it matter before you sign with any roofer.

- **It takes listed people.** The roofer must be on the program's list, and in most cases a certified evaluator sends the paperwork to the institute.
- **It is documented as the roof is built.** Photos are taken on site during the job. The old roof has to come off down to the deck.

That means you cannot decide on it after a new roof is on. If you want the designation, say so before the job starts, and check the program's directory before you sign with any roofer, including us. A designation lasts five years and then needs a new inspection.

## Do impact-resistant shingles get a discount?

Sometimes, but not by law. We found no Arkansas statute that requires a discount for Class 4 shingles. The state rule is tied to a FORTIFIED certificate, not to a shingle rating. Some insurers may offer their own roofing discount.

Class 4 is the top score in a lab test that drops a two-inch steel ball on the shingle. It is a useful rating, and it is not the whole story. The institute's own hail testing found that shingles sold as impact resistant ranged from excellent to poor.

Call your agent first. Ask what rating they credit and what proof they want.

## Does the age of your roof change your coverage?

It can. The Arkansas Insurance Department allows insurers to use a roof payment schedule on roofs seven years old or older, with proper notice to you. A roof schedule usually pays a smaller share of the replacement cost as the roof ages. Your policy has the actual numbers.

The same bulletin allows a separate wind and hail deductible. Arkansas law now requires insurers to show every deductible on your declarations page or renewal notice. A roof schedule is an endorsement, so ask your agent whether your policy has one.

A new roof is under the seven-year mark. Call your agent the week the roof is finished and ask them to update the roof age on your policy.

## Is there grant money for a stronger roof?

Not from the state yet. Act 427 created a grant program inside the Arkansas Insurance Department for FORTIFIED roof upgrades on owner-occupied homes. The act sets no grant amount and names no dedicated funding. As of early October 2026, we could not find an open application.

The act also says the home repair cannot be part of an insurance claim. So the grant, when it opens, is for owners who upgrade on their own.

There is a separate grant that is not run by the state. The Federal Home Loan Bank of Dallas has a FORTIFIED fund for income-qualified owners, offered through its member banks. Its site showed no funds available as of July 1, 2026, so ask your bank about the next round.

We will update this page when the department posts a notice.

## What should you ask your agent?

Ask four things before you choose a roof.

- Do you give a discount for a new roof, and for which materials?
- What proof do you need, and when?
- Is my roof on a payment schedule because of its age?
- Does my policy have a separate wind and hail deductible?

Write the answers down. Then compare [shingle roofing](/services/shingle-roofing) and [metal roofing](/services/metal-roofing) with real numbers in hand.
