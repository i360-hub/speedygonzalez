## Do roofers have to be licensed in Arkansas?

Yes. Since July 1, 2021, Arkansas law says no one may act as a roofing contractor on homes without a roofing contractor registration from the state, unless an exemption applies. The registration comes from the Residential Contractors Committee of the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board.

There are two common ways a roofer is legal.

| What the roofer holds | What it means | What backs it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residential roofing registration | Registered to roof homes | A surety bond filed with the Board |
| State contractor license with a roofing classification | Licensed contractor, exempt from the registration | The license itself, listed by the Board |

The Board's own form is blunt about the difference. A residential roofing registrant is not a licensed contractor. Both are legal. They are not the same thing.

## How do you look up a roofer?

Use the state's Contractor Licensing Board Search. It is free and it takes two minutes. Arkansas.gov links to it as the Licensed Contractor Search.

1. Go to [the Board's search page](http://aclb2.arkansas.gov/clbsearch.php).
2. Type the company name. Use the legal name on the contract, not just the name on the truck.
3. Read the **Valid** column. "Yes" or "Extended" means active.
4. Read the **Classifications** column. Roofing should be listed.
5. Check the **Expires** date.

If the Valid column says "Call", the Board asks you to confirm the status by phone at 501-372-4661. Do that before you sign.

The search does not show complaints. The Board says to call or email for those.

## What should a roofer be able to show you?

A name that matches the state record, a local address, and proof of insurance. Ask for them before anyone gets on your roof.

- **The exact legal name.** It should match the state search.
- **A street address.** A real shop you can drive to.
- **Proof of workers' compensation.** State law asks registered roofers with employees to carry it.
- **A written contract.** With the price, the material, and the start date.

A roofer who will not give you the legal name has told you something.

## What rights do you have after you sign?

More than most people know. Arkansas has a law for repair contracts that are paid from an insurance claim. If your insurer denies the claim, you can cancel the contract within three business days of getting that written notice.

The roofer may not start work until that right has run out. Cancel in writing, by hand or by mail to the address on the contract. The roofer then has ten days to return any deposit. Emergency work you asked for, such as a tarp, is still owed.

Door-to-door sales have their own rule. State law gives you until midnight of the third day after you sign to cancel and get your money back. Sundays and state holidays do not count. Saturdays do.

## What can a roofer not do for you?

Negotiate your claim. Arkansas does not allow public adjusters, and the Insurance Department says so on its licensing page. Your adjuster works for the insurer. We document the damage and meet the adjuster on the roof. We do not negotiate your claim.

The Insurance Department also warns homeowners after storms. Avoid any contractor who offers to waive your deductible or promises a rebate.

## Why does this matter most after a storm?

Because that is when strangers knock. A big hailstorm brings crews from other states who work a neighborhood for a week and leave. An unregistered roofer cannot use the courts to enforce a contract in Arkansas, and acting without the registration is a misdemeanor.

Two minutes on the state search sorts it out. Our guide to [Arkansas hail season](/blog/arkansas-hail-season) covers what else to do after a storm.
