## What does hail damage look like on a roof?

On asphalt shingles, hail damage is a dark spot where the granules have been knocked off. Press on it and it feels soft, like a bruise on fruit. A fresh hit looks shiny, because the asphalt under the granules has not had time to weather.

That soft spot is the real damage. It means the mat inside the shingle has cracked. Inspectors call that functional damage, because it shortens the life of the shingle.

Here is how hail looks on each part of a roof.

| Where | What hail leaves | Does it matter? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asphalt shingles | Dark, soft bruises in a random pattern | Yes, the shingle is weakened |
| Metal panels | Dents | Usually looks only, unless a seam is hit |
| Roof vents | Dents in the soft metal caps | A clue to hail size |
| Gutters and downspouts | Dents, not creases | A clue to hail size |
| Air conditioner fins | Flattened fins | A clue that hail fell |

## How do you tell hail damage from normal wear?

By the pattern and by the shape of the marks. Hail falls from high up, so the hits land at random. Wear is even. Three look-alikes fool people.

- **Blisters.** These leave small pits with steep sides, and some asphalt is missing. A hail hit is wide and shallow with gentle sides.
- **Foot traffic and tools.** These scuff the granules or crush them. Hail does not crush granules.
- **Age.** Granule loss that is even across the whole roof is normal weathering.

A few missing granules are not damage on their own. Inspectors' guidance is clear on that. The test is whether the shingle is bruised underneath.

## How big does hail have to be to hurt a roof?

About one inch, the size of a quarter. Engineering tests on eleven-year-old shingles found that one-inch ice began to damage older, thinner products. Both kinds of three-tab shingle tested were damaged at one and a quarter inches. The thicker laminated shingle held until one and a half inches.

The National Weather Service calls hail severe at one inch.

Smaller hail still counts over time. Research from the insurance industry's lab found that weathered shingles hit by small hail were up to ten times more easily damaged by later large hail than new shingles. Cold matters too. Inspectors' guidance says shingles are much more vulnerable to hail below about 50 degrees.

## What should you check from the ground?

Check the soft metal first. It dents before anything else, and it gives a rough idea of how big the hail was.

1. **Gutters and downspouts.** Look for round dents. Granules in the gutter alone do not prove hail, because worn shingles shed them too.
2. **Roof vents and flashing.** The aluminum caps dent easily.
3. **The air conditioner.** The thin fins flatten easily under hail.
4. **Window screens and the mailbox.** If they were hit, the roof was too.
5. **Ceilings inside.** Any new stain needs a call.

Take photos and write down the date of the storm. Then stop. Do not climb up. Storm debris and loose granules make a roof slick.

## What does hail do to a metal roof?

It dents it. Inspectors' guidance says hail may dent metal panels but seldom causes damage that makes the roof leak. The exception is a hit on a seam or a piece of trim.

Dents still matter for a claim. Some policies treat dents as cosmetic. Ask your agent how yours reads before storm season. Our guide on [how long a metal roof lasts](/blog/how-long-does-a-metal-roof-last) covers what to check after hail.

## When is hail most likely in Hot Springs?

March through June. In federal storm records from 1955 through 2025, about seven in ten reports of hail one inch or larger in Garland County fell in those four months. April is the peak.

It can be severe. On June 14, 2023, the National Weather Service reported hail up to softball size on the south side of Hot Springs, including around Lake Hamilton. Two days later, it reported baseball-size hail in Hot Springs Village.
