## What is the difference between architectural and 3-tab shingles?

A 3-tab shingle is one flat layer cut into three tabs. An architectural shingle is two or more layers bonded together, so it is thicker, heavier, and built to hold in higher wind. Both are asphalt shingles, and both go on the same way.

You may also hear architectural shingles called dimensional or laminated shingles. It is the same product.

Here is how the two compare.

| | 3-tab | Architectural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layers | One | Two or more |
| Weight for every 100 square feet | 200 to 250 pounds | 250 to 300 pounds |
| Wind it is designed for | Up to about 60 mph | About 70 to 110 mph |
| Life on a national chart | About 20 years | About 30 years |
| Life we see in Garland County | 15 to 20 years | 20 to 25 years |
| Look | Flat, even rows | Layered, shadowed |

The weight and wind figures come from the home inspectors' association. They are general numbers, not a rating for any one product.

## Which one lasts longer in Arkansas?

Architectural shingles do. National inspection charts give them about ten more years than 3-tab shingles under mild conditions. In Garland County, we see about five more years from an architectural roof than from a 3-tab roof.

The national chart numbers are higher than ours for a reason. They assume normal wear and no extreme weather. Arkansas has long hot summers and spring hail, and both shorten the life of asphalt. Our guide on [how long a roof lasts in Arkansas](/blog/how-long-does-a-roof-last-arkansas) explains what moves the number.

## What do the wind ratings mean?

They tell you how the shingle did in a lab when it was new. Two tests are common. The Arkansas residential code calls for the second one, and accepts the first for shingles the second does not cover.

- **The fan test.** A fan blows on the shingles for two hours. Class A passes at 60 mph, Class D at 90, and Class F at 110.
- **The uplift test.** Engineers measure how hard wind pulls on a sealed shingle and compare it with how well the shingle holds. The classes are D, G, and H, from lowest to highest.

Both ratings are for new shingles. The insurance industry's research lab notes that these tests do not account for weathering or age. A rating is a starting point, not a forecast.

## What is a Class 4 impact rating?

It is the top score in a lab hail test. A steel ball is dropped on the shingle twice in the same spot. For Class 4, the ball is two inches across. The shingle passes if no crack shows on the back.

Surface dents and lost granules do not count as a failure in that test. That is one reason lab ratings and real hail differ. When the same research lab ran its own hail tests on shingles sold as impact resistant, results ranged from excellent to poor.

So ask two questions about an impact-rated shingle. What class is it, and how did it score in the lab's hail ratings? Then ask your insurance agent what they credit. Our guide on [new roofs and home insurance](/blog/new-roof-home-insurance-arkansas) covers the Arkansas rules.

## When does a 3-tab shingle still make sense?

On simple, low-risk roofs. A 3-tab shingle costs less up front, and it is fine on a shed, a rental, or a low roof with no trees over it.

It is also the right choice for a repair on a roof that already has 3-tab shingles. Mixing the two types leaves a patch that shows.

For a home you plan to keep, we recommend architectural shingles. We install them on almost every house we roof.

## Which shingle fits your house?

Think about three things.

- **How long you will stay.** A longer stay favors the thicker shingle.
- **What hangs over the roof.** Heavy tree cover and hail both favor it too.
- **The shape of the roof.** A steep roof with valleys shows the layered look best.
