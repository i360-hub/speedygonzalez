## How long does a metal roof last?

A metal roof lasts 40 to 70 years in Arkansas. That is our number, and it sits inside what neutral sources say. The home inspectors' association chart gives metal 40 to 80 years. A U.S. Department of Energy guide says a metal roof can last up to 50 years or more.

Sources differ, and none says which kind of metal roof it means.

| Source | What it says | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home inspectors' chart | 40 to 80 years | Metal roofs in general |
| U.S. Department of Energy guide | Up to 50 years or more | Metal roofs in general |
| Metal roofing trade group | 30 to 50 years or more | Metal roofs in general |
| Speedy Gonzalez Roofing | 40 to 70 years | Roofs in Garland County |

No neutral source we found gives separate numbers for exposed fastener panels and standing seam. What they do agree on is which parts wear out first.

## What fails first on a metal roof?

The small parts. A metal panel can outlast two shingle roofs. The screws, the sealant, and the flashing around it do not last as long, and they are where leaks start.

- **Fasteners.** Metal grows and shrinks with the heat. That movement can loosen screws and back them out over time.
- **Washers.** Exposed fastener panels use screws with a rubber washer. The washer is the seal.
- **Sealant.** Inspectors' guidance says sealant eventually dries, shrinks, and cracks, and has to be renewed.
- **Seams and flashing.** These are the joints. Inspectors' guidance calls seams a weak point in the system, and says of flashing that its connections are what tend to fail.
- **Finish.** Sun fades the color over time. Inspectors' guidance also calls corrosion an important way metal roofing fails.

A metal industry study of low-slope, unpainted standing seam roofs up to 35 years old pointed the same way. It projected that the seam sealant, not the steel, will decide when those roofs are finished, and put that at 60 years or more.

## Does the type of panel matter?

Yes. There are two common types, and they hold water out in different ways.

Exposed fastener panels are screwed through the face of the metal. Every screw is a hole with a washer over it, so there are many small seals to keep an eye on. These panels cost less and work well on shops, barns, and porches.

Standing seam panels hide the fasteners under a raised seam. There are no screw holes on the surface. That is why we recommend standing seam for most homes.

## Does hail ruin a metal roof?

Rarely. Inspectors' guidance says hail may dent metal panels but seldom causes damage that affects how the roof works. A dent is a mark. A leak is a failure. Hail does more harm when it hits a seam or trim.

Thickness matters. A federal energy lab notes that heavier gauge metal resists hail better than lighter gauge metal.

Garland County sees large hail. In June 2023, the National Weather Service reported hail up to softball size on the south side of Hot Springs. After a storm like that, check a metal roof for dents at the seams and around the vents.

Our guide on [what hail damage looks like](/blog/what-hail-damage-looks-like) shows what to look for.

## How often should a metal roof be checked?

Twice a year, and after any severe storm. That is the National Roofing Contractors Association's recommendation for any roof: an inspection each spring and fall, plus one after severe weather.

A good check covers four things.

1. Screws that have backed out or lost a washer.
2. Sealant that has cracked at flashing and trim.
3. Debris packed into the valleys.
4. Scratches or rust at cut edges.

Do the looking from the ground or the attic. A metal roof is slick when wet. If you already have a drip, see our guide on [how to find a leak in a metal roof](/blog/find-leak-metal-roof).

## Is a metal roof worth it?

It is if you plan to stay. A metal roof costs more up front and lasts two to three times as long as shingles. Over 50 years, that is usually one metal roof against two or three shingle roofs.

It is not free of upkeep. Plan on having the fasteners and sealant looked at as the roof ages. For the full comparison, read [metal vs shingles in Arkansas](/blog/metal-vs-shingles-arkansas).
