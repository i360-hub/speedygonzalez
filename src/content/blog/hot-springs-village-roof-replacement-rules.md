## Do you need a POA permit to replace a roof in Hot Springs Village?

Yes. The Hot Springs Village Property Owners' Association requires a permit for roofing on existing homes. Its residential rules say any change to the outside of a home, including the roof, needs a permit. The POA permit portal lists replacement roofing as its own permit type.

This is a POA permit, not a city one. The Village is not a city. It sits in Garland and Saline counties, and the POA handles building permits inside the gates.

Here is the short version.

| Question | What the POA rules say |
| --- | --- |
| Is a permit required? | Yes, for roofing on existing homes |
| Who reviews it? | The Architectural Control Committee, case by case |
| What colors? | Earth tones |
| Metal roofs? | Standing seam or flat-panel batten, concealed fasteners |
| What if work starts without one? | Fines, and possible removal or changes |
| Who to call | Permitting and Inspections, 501-922-5562 |

## What colors are allowed?

Earth tones. The list in the rules includes browns, tans, warm grays, greens such as moss, whites, and some gray blues. Black and charcoal are not on it, so ask before you order. The committee decides on the actual sample.

Send the color name with the permit application. If you are replacing like for like, say so. It gives the reviewer less to question.

## Are metal roofs allowed?

Yes, with limits. The POA's replacement roofing application has a page of roof standards, and it is specific about metal.

- **Panel type.** Standing seam or flat-panel batten systems are required. Metal shingles that look like traditional roofing are permitted.
- **Fasteners.** Concealed fasteners are required. Exposed fasteners are not permitted.
- **No corrugated panels.** They are not allowed.
- **Finish.** Matte or low sheen, with a fade-resistant coating.
- **Thickness.** 26 gauge is the minimum, and 24 gauge is recommended.

So the screw-down panels common on shops and barns are out for a Village home. Standing seam is in. Get the approval in writing before any material is bought.

## What if you live in a townhome?

Then two groups have a say. Townhomes are under the Hot Springs Village Townhouse Association as well as the POA. The Townhouse Association reviews the roof first, and the POA issues the permit after.

The Townhouse Association rules are stricter.

- **Approval comes first.** Re-roofing must be approved before work is done.
- **No wood shakes.** They are not allowed as a replacement because of fire risk.
- **Your neighbors sign.** A change in roof material or color needs the court representative and the other owners in your building to agree. If you are the only owner in your building re-roofing, the application asks for their signed consent.
- **There is a deadline.** The application says work must be finished within 90 days of approval.

Roofing samples are kept at the Townhouse Association office. The number is 501-922-1375.

## What happens if a roof goes on without a permit?

The POA can fine you and can require changes. Its policy guide says work done without a permit is subject to immediate or ongoing fines, and to removal or modification after the committee reviews it. The fee schedule has its own line for construction with no permit.

A permit takes less time than that argument. Ask your roofer who is filing it, and ask for a copy.

## Why do Village roofs need work so often?

Trees and storms. The Village kept its woods, so pines and oaks hang over most homes. Needles pack the valleys, hold water, and wear the roof from the top down.

The Village has also taken direct hits. The National Weather Service reported baseball-size hail in Hot Springs Village on June 16, 2023. On March 14, 2024, it reported an EF2 tornado there, with baseball-size hail and many homes damaged by falling trees.

After a storm like that, the permit still applies. Tarping to stop a leak is one thing. The new roof is another. Our guide on [what to do when your roof is leaking](/blog/roof-leaking-what-to-do) covers the first hour.

## How do the city and county rules compare?

They are different offices with different rules. Inside Hot Springs city limits, the city issues roof permits. In the rest of Garland County, the county generally issues none. Our guide to [roof permits in Hot Springs](/blog/roof-permit-hot-springs) lays out each one.
