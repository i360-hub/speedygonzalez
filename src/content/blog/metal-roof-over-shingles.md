## Can you put a metal roof over shingles?

Yes, in some cases. The Arkansas residential code allows new roofing over an existing roof when it is installed to the maker's instructions. It does not allow it over a roof that is water soaked or badly worn, or over a roof that already has two or more layers.

So one sound layer of shingles can be covered. Anything else has to come off.

Here is the short test.

| Your roof now | Can metal go over it? |
| --- | --- |
| One layer of shingles, dry and flat | Yes, if installed to the maker's instructions |
| Two or more layers | No, tear off first |
| Wet, soft, or sagging in places | No, tear off and fix the deck |
| Leaks you have not found | No, find the cause first |

## What does the Arkansas code say?

Here is what it says. Arkansas uses the 2021 International Residential Code for homes and adopted its roofing chapter with no changes.

- **A replacement is a tear-off by default.** The old roofing comes off down to the deck.
- **Covering the old roof is the exception.** It is allowed when the new roof goes on to the maker's approved instructions.
- **Three cases are barred.** A roof that is water soaked or too worn to be a base, a roof of slate or tile, and a roof with two or more layers.
- **Standing seam can count as its own system.** The code says a complete and separate roofing system, such as standing seam metal that carries its own loads to the structure, does not require the old roof to be removed. The three barred cases still apply.

The structure also has to carry the load. Metal helps there. A metal industry manual puts metal at 40 to 120 pounds for every 100 square feet, against 200 to 350 for shingles.

## What are the reasons to go over the old roof?

Less mess and less labor. The old shingles stay put, so there is far less to tear off and haul away. The ridge caps and any overhanging shingles still come off. The metal industry's own manual says a metal roof may be installed over existing shingles.

It also keeps the house covered during the job. The old roof stays on until the panels go over it.

## What are the reasons to tear off?

You get to see the wood. That is the main one. A cover-up hides the deck, and the roofing contractors' association warns that a look from the attic may not show how bad the top of the deck is.

There are three more.

- **Rot stays in place.** Soft decking under old shingles does not get fixed.
- **Hail has more to work with.** Inspectors' guidance says layers of old roofing give poor support against hail.
- **Moisture can get trapped.** Without the right underlayment and air space, the underside of the metal can sweat.

The roofing contractors' association does not recommend a cover-up over a damaged deck. Neither do we. The Department of Energy's metal roof guide goes further and says to remove all the existing roofing on a replacement.

## What has to be done right if you go over shingles?

Four things. They come from the metal industry's installation manual and from home inspectors' guidance.

1. **Fix the old roof first.** Repair damage and clear off moss and debris.
2. **Make it flat.** Metal needs a smooth surface, or every hump shows through.
3. **Add a full layer of underlayment.** Inspectors' guidance calls it especially important when metal goes over an old roof.
4. **Use wood strips where the panel calls for them.** They hold the panels and let air move underneath.

The screws must reach the deck, not just the old shingles.

## What do we usually do?

We usually tear off. Rotten decking is common on older Hot Springs homes, and you cannot see it through shingles. A tear-off lets us photograph the wood, replace what is soft, and start clean.

We will go over a single sound layer when the roof and the panel allow it. We tell you which one your roof is after we inspect it.

One more point. A FORTIFIED roof designation requires the old roofing to be removed to the deck. If you want that designation for your insurance, a cover-up is off the table. Our guide on [new roofs and home insurance](/blog/new-roof-home-insurance-arkansas) explains why.
